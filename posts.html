A website is not finished when it goes live.

I’ve seen many websites look great at launch, but a few months later, even small updates become difficult.

Adding a new page takes too long.

The design starts becoming inconsistent.

The structure becomes harder to manage.

And eventually, the website stops supporting the business the way it should.
That is why I don’t see a website as a one-time project.
It needs to be built in a way that makes future updates easier, keeps the brand consistent, and allows the website to grow without creating unnecessary problems.

A strong website should help a business:
→ Update content easily
→ Add new pages faster
→ Maintain a consistent look
→ Improve the user experience
→ Turn more visitors into customers
Tools and platforms will continue to change.
But the fundamentals will always matter:

Good strategy.
Clean structure.
Thoughtful design.
A better experience for users.
Launching the website is only the beginning.

The real goal is to build something that keeps working and delivering value long after it goes live.

After working with 450+ firms across finance, from PE funds to the companies they acquire, here are the 7 layers of a successful raise.

Most GPs obsess over one layer.
They chase meetings.
But a raise is a stack.

And a problem at the bottom shows up as a symptom at the top.

You can't fix a layer you can't see.

So here's the full stack, bottom to top:

1/ Data layer

LP sourcing, allocator databases, mandate signals.

Where targeting is won or lost. The wrong list means pitching allocators who finished deploying a year ago.

Preqin, PitchBook, Dakota Marketplace

2/ Qualification layer

Segmentation by asset class, check size, allocation timing.

Not every LP gets the same outreach. A pension and a family office should never receive the same first email.

Fintrx, With Intelligence + custom scoring

3/ Narrative layer

Positioning. Why this strategy, and why this team is the one to run it.

The layer where most raises fail.

Collateral Partners

4/ Materials layer

Pitch materials, capability decks, DDQ support, fundraise PR, LP-portal infrastructure.

The first thing LPs study, and the last thing that should be built.

Collateral Partners

5/ Presence layer

Website, LinkedIn, first page of Google.

LPs check all three before answering the intro email.

Webflow, LinkedIn

6/ Relationship layer

CRM, follow-up, what LPs hear from you between meetings.

LPs rarely commit to a manager they only hear from during a raise.

Affinity (CRM), Dynamo Software

7/ Closing layer

Diligence materials, subscription docs, the final conversation.

Where everyone wants to start. The six layers below decide how it goes.

iCapital, Anduin Transactions + your IR team

Skip a layer and the whole thing leaks.

Which layer is a bottleneck at your firm?
